HW2 — Zone Build and HTTP Forensics

Due: before Week 3 class · Points: 100 · Weight: part of the 35% homework component Effort: ~8–9 hours including the beginner bridge, plus an elapsed wait of at least one TTL — start on day one Individual: every student configures, tests, and submits their own zone. The evidence and the writing must be yours.

The point of this assignment, in one sentence. Take the domain you bought in HW1 and make it a system you operate — configured deliberately, changed on a plan, and diagnosed from evidence rather than from guesses.

Everything you produce here is reused. Nothing in this assignment is throwaway.

Use the public weekly reference, not private lecture/speaker notes. For evidence-standard parts 6–7 without a personal AI session, critique the HW2 supplied DNS change-plan draft, cite it and connect the claim to your permitted cutover evidence.

2. Prerequisites

Before you start, confirm all five. If any fails, fix it first — the tasks depend on it.

  1. You control a domain or assigned course subdomain (HW1 evidence). Registrar access is only necessary for your own registration.
  2. You know who hosts your DNS, and it is the same place you are editing records. Confirm with dig NS yourdomain.example +short.
  3. dig works. dig -v prints a version. Installation table in lab.md.
  4. curl works. curl --version.
  5. Node 24 LTS, for the supplied fault server: node --version.

Domain trouble? The hardship path from Week 1 still applies. Contact the instructor privately; you will be issued a delegated subdomain of the course domain with record-editing rights or an authorized change workflow. No explanation is required and there is no grade difference. For a leaf in the shared course zone, document parent delegation instead of claiming an independent child zone; use the zone apex for NS/SOA and submit an export of only your authorized records.

3. Tasks

Task 1 — Trace and explain the delegation path (10 pts)

Run and capture:

dig +trace yourdomain.example A
dig +trace www.yourdomain.example A

Then annotate the first transcript. For every group in the output — starting with the root-hints bootstrap and ending with the terminal authoritative answer — write one line naming (a) which server answered, (b) whether it gave a referral or an answer, and (c) how you can tell from the output. The number of groups depends on your domain; a .co.uk or a delegated subdomain has more than a .com.

Deliverable: evidence/01-dig-trace.txt (raw) + a section in your report with the annotation.

If +trace stalls, use dig -4 +trace and say in your report that you did, and why.

Task 2 — Build and export your zone, with a reason for every line (25 pts)

Configure your zone to hold at minimum:

RecordRequirement
An address-bearing nameWhatever your Task 3 decision requires: A/AAAA at the apex, a CNAME at www, or your host's ALIAS. Use what your hosting target actually needs.
AAAAOr a written justification for omitting it
CNAMEAt least one subdomain, e.g. docs or app
TXTThe non-secret D2L ownership nonce or a real service verification string. No mail service or paid account is required.
CAARequired. Name the CA you intend to use in Week 4
MXConfigure it, or publish Null MX (MX 0 ., RFC 7505) and say why
cutoverAn A record at TTL 3600, value 203.0.113.10, DNS only / unproxied. This is Task 5's disposable subject.
NS / SOARead what your host generated; you do not author these and are not graded on them

If your DNS host is Cloudflare, every record you create must be "DNS only" (grey cloud), not "Proxied" (orange cloud). A proxied record ignores your TTL, reports Auto, and returns Cloudflare's addresses instead of yours — so your TTL evidence and your cutover measurements will both be measuring something you did not configure. Other proxying hosts behave the same way.

203.0.113.0/24 is TEST-NET-3 (RFC 5737): documentation-only, non-routable. Records pointing there will never serve traffic. That is deliberate for cutover, which exists to be changed and rolled back, not to work.

Export records from your DNS host. If it has no BIND-zone export, submit the provider's JSON/CLI export plus your BIND-style annotated representation; for a course zone export only your authorized subset, not other students' records. Sanitize any non-public account metadata before committing. Commit the raw export, then a second, annotated copy in which every record carries a rationale and a TTL justification as comments. The worked example below is the expected depth: a few lines each, not a single clause.

Worked example of the expected depth — this is the bar:

; ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
@   3600  IN  A    198.51.100.42
;   WHY:  Apex is primary (see Task 3). Points at the address my hosting platform
;         assigned to the CampusPulse container. Chosen over CNAME because an apex
;         CNAME is illegal and my host does not support ALIAS. If it did, I would
;         have used ALIAS so the platform could move me without my involvement.
;   TTL:  3600. Steady state. The address is stable and changing it is a planned
;         event, not an emergency. Long enough to survive a brief authoritative
;         outage; short enough that a planned cutover only needs one hour of
;         pre-lowering rather than a day.

docs  3600  IN  CNAME  campuspulse-docs.pages.dev.
;   WHY:  Docs are served by a platform that assigns me a hostname, not an
;         address. A CNAME is legal here because `docs` is a subdomain, and it
;         lets the platform re-address without touching my zone.
;   TTL:  3600. I do not expect to repoint this. Note the effective delay on any
;         change is governed by THIS record's TTL, not by the target's — each
;         link in the chain is cached independently.

@   3600  IN  CAA  0 issue "letsencrypt.org"
;   WHY:  Restricts issuance to Let's Encrypt, which is what HW4's ACME client
;         will use. Without this record, and with no CAA on any ancestor, there
;         is no applicable CAA restriction and any public CA may issue for me.
;   TTL:  3600. Changing this is rare and always deliberate. Note: if I switch
;         CA in Week 4 I must change this FIRST or issuance will be refused.

cutover  3600  IN  A  203.0.113.10
;   WHY:  Disposable. Exists only as Task 5's cutover subject. TEST-NET-3, so it
;         serves nothing and breaking it cannot break my site. DNS only, not
;         proxied, so the TTL I set is the TTL that is served.
;   TTL:  3600 deliberately, so that lowering it to 60 requires a real one-hour
;         wait before the cutover is safe. A record already at 60 would make the
;         planning exercise vacuous.
; ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A rationale should go beyond "this points the website at the server." A rationale names the alternative you rejected and the consequence of the TTL you chose.

You are not graded on the NS and SOA records your host generated. Read them, annotate the SOA minimum field with what it controls, and move on.

Deliverable: evidence/02-zone-raw.txt (the unmodified export) and evidence/02-zone-annotated.txt (your annotated copy).

Task 3 — Decide apex vs www, and defend it (10 pts)

This task is a documented decision, not an implementation. You are not required to build a working redirect this week — HTTPS redirects land in Week 4, when you have a certificate. If you have already implemented one, include curl -sIL evidence for extra credibility; if you have not, describe what you will build.

Pick one:

  • Apex-primary — yourdomain.example serves the site; www redirects to it.
  • www-primary — www.yourdomain.example serves the site; the apex redirects.

Write 150–300 words covering all four:

  1. Which you chose and how it is implemented in your zone (record types, literally).
  2. What your DNS host offers for the apex — ALIAS, ANAME, CNAME flattening, or nothing — and how that constrained you. Name the feature your host actually has; do not describe a generic capability.
  3. How the non-primary name redirects — the status code you chose and why. Design is sufficient; state whether it is built yet.
  4. One consequence of your choice you would have to live with — cookie scope, cert SANs, or an operational limitation.

Deliverable: a section in your report. Include dig output for both names, even if one of them returns NODATA — say so and explain why that is the expected result for your design.

Task 4 — Prove resolution from two independent resolvers (10 pts)

Query at least two resolvers you do not control, plus the authoritative source. Use the name that actually carries your A record — the one you chose in Task 3. If you went www-primary, that is www.yourdomain.example, not the apex.

dig @1.1.1.1 <your-A-record-name> A
dig @8.8.8.8 <your-A-record-name> A
dig @9.9.9.9 <your-A-record-name> A          # a third is free evidence
dig @<your-authoritative-ns> <your-A-record-name> A +norecurse

Querying a name with no A record returns NOERROR with ANSWER: 0 — NODATA — and there is no TTL to do arithmetic on. That is a correct result for the wrong question.

In your report, state for each: the flags, the TTL, and — for the authoritative one — the fact that aa is set. Then compute the apparent cache age for one resolver:

authoritative TTL (measured at the same time) − resolver-reported TTL
    = seconds since that resolver's cache last refreshed

Show the arithmetic. Measure the authoritative TTL yourself in the same session — do not assume the value you configured is the value being served, because a proxying host may override it.

Call the result an apparent cache age. A resolver may cap long TTLs to its own ceiling, prefetch just before expiry, or serve stale on failure, so the subtraction is a very good estimate rather than a guarantee.

If your primary name is a CNAME, say which RRset you did the arithmetic on. The CNAME and the A it points to are cached independently and usually have different TTLs.

Deliverable: evidence/04-resolvers.txt + the analysis.

Your laptop is one useful observation, not evidence that all clients see the same answer. Compare it with authoritative and independent resolver evidence.

Task 5 — Plan and execute a cutover, then roll it back (20 pts)

This is the largest single block of marks and the part a model cannot do for you.

Use the cutover record from Task 2 — the disposable A record at TTL 3600 pointing at TEST-NET-3, DNS only. Do not run this exercise against a record your site depends on, and do not use a record already at TTL 60: with no waiting period the planning arithmetic is vacuous and the task loses its marks.

5a. The plan, written before you change anything

CUTOVER PLAN
  Record            : cutover.yourdomain.example (A)
  Current value     : 203.0.113.10   <- the rollback target — write it down FIRST
  New value         : 198.51.100.77
  Current TTL       : 3600
  TTL lowered to    : 60 at <timestamp>
  Earliest safe cut : <lowered-at + 3600 s>   <- and say why
  Change at         : <timestamp>
  Verification      : dig @1.1.1.1 ..., dig @8.8.8.8 ..., dig +trace ...
  Success criteria  : both selected resolvers return 198.51.100.77
  Rollback trigger  : the AUTHORITATIVE answer is wrong, or the service behind it
                      fails health checks. NOT "a resolver is still stale" —
                      expected cache lag is not evidence that the cutover failed.
  Rollback action   : restore 203.0.113.10; expected live within 60 s
  Rollback owner    : <who decides>

The Earliest safe cut line is graded. If it is not at least the old TTL after the lowering, you have not understood the mechanism and the task loses points regardless of how clean the transcripts are.

5b. Execute it

Capture, with timestamps, in this order:

  1. Before-state from both resolvers.
  2. The TTL lowering — a screenshot (evidence/05-ttl-lowered.png) or a provider API call/audit-log entry — and the wall-clock time.
  3. The record change, and the wall-clock time.
  4. Verification queries, repeated, until both selected resolvers show the new value.
  5. The elapsed time from change to visibility at those two resolvers.

"Visible at the two resolvers you chose" is not the same as "visible everywhere." Two anycast services are a sample, not a census. Say so in your write-up.

5c. Roll back

Restore the original value. Capture verification the same way and record the elapsed rollback time.

5d. Reflect

Two to four sentences: was your TTL choice right? If you had to do this cutover under incident pressure at 2 a.m., what would you change about the plan?

Deliverable: evidence/05-cutover.txt (all transcripts, timestamped) + plan and reflection in the report.

Task 6 — Diagnose three supplied HTTP failures (20 pts)

Three broken behaviours are supplied in weeks/week02/samples/http-faults/. Each is a real, runnable fault, not a description of one.

node weeks/week02/samples/http-faults/fault-server.mjs
FaultArtifactThe reported symptom
1fault-1-host-header.txt"www 404s but DNS is right. Not propagated yet?"

2Bfault-2-redirect-chain.txt parts B & C"Our POST silently stopped creating things"
3fault-3-x-forwarded-proto.txt"Redirects forever, but only in production"

Fault 2 contains two independent defects. Treat 2A and 2B as separate faults and produce all four outputs below for each. That is four faults' worth of write-up in total.

For each fault, produce all four:

  1. The annotated request/response pair — the specific lines that carry the evidence, with your annotation on each.
  2. The diagnosis — one or two sentences naming the actual cause.
  3. The confirming command — the single command that would distinguish your diagnosis from the next most plausible one, and what result would confirm it.
  4. The fix — what you would change, and where (app, proxy, or DNS).

You must also reproduce at least one of the three yourself by running the fault server and capturing your own transcript. Say which one and include your capture.

Deliverable: evidence/06-http-faults.txt, containing all three transcripts — your own capture for the one you reproduced, and the supplied transcripts for the other two, so everything is in one file. The four-part write-up per fault goes in your report.

Fault 2B contains a trap that will catch you if you are careless. Two of the supplied captures are the same request with one flag different, and they produce opposite conclusions. If your diagnosis does not mention it, you missed it.

Deliverable: evidence/06-http-faults.txt + the four-part write-up per fault.

Task 7 — The evidence standard (5 pts)

Every submission in this course carries the same eight parts. Items 5–8 are the ones students lose marks on.

  1. Commit SHA or release tag for the repo state you submitted.
  2. Reproduction commands and your exported zone.
  3. Raw sanitized evidence — the evidence/ files above.
  4. Annotated interpretation in your own words — the report.
  5. At least one deliberate failure and its diagnosis. Suggested, and genuinely instructive: query a name before you create it, observe the cached NXDOMAIN, read the SOA returned in the negative response's AUTHORITY section, and predict when it will clear. The negative-cache lifetime is the lesser of that SOA record's own TTL and its minimum field (RFC 2308 §5). Then verify your prediction.
  6. AI-use log — what you generated, accepted, rejected, and how you verified it.
  7. One challenged AI claim, with the evidence that rejected it.
  8. Redaction attestation — no live secrets, tokens, cookies, session identifiers, or API keys. Replace values; keep their shape.

Redaction, specifically for this assignment. DNS output is generally safe. Two things are not: your DNS host's API token (never paste one) and any Set-Cookie value you capture from a real site. Replace the cookie value with REDACTED and keep the attributes — the attributes are the evidence.

Plus one mandatory wall-clock wait you cannot compress. Task 5 requires lowering the TTL and then waiting at least the old TTL before cutting over. The waiting is not work, but it is on the calendar, and it is the reason this assignment cannot be done in one sitting.

8. The non-generatable component

An AI can write you a zone file in four seconds. It cannot do any of this:

  • It cannot log in to your registrar. Delegation is an account you control.
  • It cannot know your change window. The TTL encodes your tolerance for being wrong, and only you know what that is.
  • It cannot watch a cache expire. Task 5's elapsed times are measurements of a live distributed system, taken by you, at a time it cannot observe.
  • It cannot roll back. Restoring a value you failed to write down first is not something any model can rescue you from — which is why the plan is graded before the execution.
  • It cannot tell you which of the two Fault 2 captures is lying. Both are real transcripts of the same server. Deciding which one reproduces the user's problem is a judgement about methodology, and it is the single most transferable skill in this assignment.

If you use a model for this — and you should — the honest split is: let it draft your rationale prose, then check every technical claim against your own dig output. Log the corrections. That log is worth marks.
